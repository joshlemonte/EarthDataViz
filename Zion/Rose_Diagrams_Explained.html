Why you can’t just average directions

Directions wrap around. 350° and 10° are only 20° apart, but their arithmetic mean is 180°, which points the opposite way. The fix is the vector mean: add up each reading’s north and east components, then turn the total back into an angle. The length of that total, divided by n, is R̄: 1 when every reading agrees, near 0 when they cancel out.

Directions or lines?

A cross-bed dip direction has a true direction: 200° is not the same as 020°. A joint strike does not: 040° and 220° describe the same line. That is axial data. Bin it from 0 to 180° and draw each petal twice, on opposite sides, so the rose is symmetric.

Plot the same joint strikes as if they were directions and half the rose disappears, which looks like a preferred direction that isn’t there.

What geologists use them for

Paleocurrents and paleowinds

Cross-bed foresets dip downstream or downwind. Navajo Sandstone foresets dipping mostly southward record Jurassic winds from the north. Ripple marks, flute casts and imbricated pebbles work the same way.

Joints, fractures and faults

Strike orientations reveal the stress field that broke the rock. In hydrogeology, fracture sets often steer which way groundwater moves.

Glacial ice flow

Striations and the long axes of elongated clasts line up with the direction the ice moved.

Wind roses

Meteorologists and airport planners use the same plot for prevailing winds. One catch: a wind rose shows where wind comes from, while a cross-bed shows where it went.

Four things to check before you trust one

Enough readings for the bins

With 8 readings in 10° bins, most petals are noise. Use wider bins or more data, and put n and the bin width in the caption.

Axial or directional?

Strikes, striations and clast long axes are lines. Plotting them as directions draws half a rose and invents a preferred direction.

Petal length vs. area

A petal twice as long has four times the area, so big bins look more dominant than they are. Equal-area petals fix that; either way, say which you used.

One north for everyone

Clino and Rockd set to true north are fine. A hand compass needs the magnetic declination added, about 10° east around Zion; check the NOAA calculator for the current value.
